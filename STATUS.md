# Monarchy 0.1.0 — release status

This file is the release contract for 0.1.0. It lists what the build actually
does, how each capability is proven, and what is deliberately **not** claimed.
Anything not listed under "Implemented and verified" is absent, not partial.

Verified on this tree with `scripts/ci.sh` — a fresh configure, a build that
emits no warnings, and every suite → **26/26 suites pass**. `scripts/ci.sh
--conformance` goes further: it provisions a throwaway domain, starts the daemon
on free ports and drives the running DC with impacket's own DRSR and LSAD NDR
clients, so a reply only this tree's encoder understands fails the check.

## 2. Not implemented — not claimed, not stubbed

- **SAMR beyond the calls above**: `SamrSetInformationDomain` beyond classes 1
  and 12 (the general and logon information classes are reported but not
  settable over RPC, and there is no fine-grained password policy — a PSO on a
  user or group — so the domain policy is the only one applied);
  `SamrQueryInformationUser2` beyond classes 1–14, 16, 17, 20 and 21 — the
  password and internal classes 18, 23–26 are refused with
  `STATUS_INVALID_INFO_CLASS` rather than answered with a truncated arm, which a
  client reads as `STATUS_BUFFER_TOO_SMALL`;
  `SamrSetInformationGroup` classes 1–3 and
  `SamrSetInformationAlias` classes 1–2 (renaming a group or alias and setting
  its SE_GROUP_* attribute set have no directory attribute behind them, so they
  are refused rather than accepted and dropped); and writing to `BUILTIN`: the
  domain (S-1-5-32) is enumerated, its sixteen well-known aliases can be opened,
  queried and have their members read, but a create, delete, rename or member
  change there returns `STATUS_NOT_SUPPORTED`, because AD keeps that domain in a
  naming context of its own and this server derives it from the SID table Lsad
  already resolves rather than storing it. The RID master's pool is not used either: a new
  account's RID comes from the store's own counter, so the role object names its
  owner but carries no `rIDAvailablePool`.
- **Trusts are a working inter-realm channel, with these edges**: the trust,
  its SID, direction, names and the inter-realm keys derived from its clear
  secret are stored, reported and rewritable, and two DCs that both hold the
  trust now authenticate across it — `scripts/trust-conformance.py` creates it
  over lsarpc from Samba's pidl client, reads it back at levels 5 and 6,
  enumerates it and opens it by name, then MIT walks the trust on its own:
  `kinit Administrator@<peer>` in one realm, `kvno ldap/<host>@<this realm>`
  from the other. What is still missing: `LsarQueryForestTrustInfo` has nothing
  to return (no forest trust, no transited realms beyond one hop, no SID
  filtering or selective authentication), the internal-encryption query levels
  are refused with `STATUS_INVALID_INFO_CLASS` rather than guessed at, nothing
  rolls the trust password automatically the way a DC does every 30 days, and
  the channel has been driven against Samba's client and MIT's KDC, not against
  a Windows DC. `LsarSetInformationPolicy`
  sets the modification time and the audit-full flag and refuses the rest (the
  domain name and SID are reported, not settable), as does
  `LsarSetDomainInformationPolicy` (the Kerberos lifetimes are reported, not
  settable). `LsarCreateAccount` answers `STATUS_NOT_SUPPORTED`: MS-LSAD 3.1.4.5.1
  has accounts created through SAMR.
- **LSAD state that is not part of the store**: LSA secrets and private data live
  in the serving process (they are not persisted, and nothing encrypts them at
  rest), and the per-account system-access mask is per handle rather than
  written to the directory.
- **DRSR link schedules**: `DsReplicaMod` (opnum 7) changes a registered
  link's address, replica flags and schedule, and the schedule now gates the
  pull: a link whose flags carry `DRSUAPI_DRS_PER_SYNC` is pulled only inside the
  15-minute slots its `REPLTIMES` sets (Sunday 00:00 UTC first, most significant
  bit first, re-checked at least daily), while a link without that flag keeps
  `replicaIntervalSeconds`. `DsReplicaGetInfo` NEIGHBORS has no schedule member
  in MS-DRSR, so the schedule cannot be read back over the wire, and no per-link
  sync history is kept: that reply's USNs, timestamps and failure counters are
  zero because they are unmeasured, not because a sync succeeded. `DsReplicaDel` with
  `DRS_NO_SOURCE` (which expunges a whole NC) is
  refused, and the `UpdateRefs` notification AD sends to the peer on removal is
  not sent. A source added at level 1 of `DsReplicaAdd` carries no source DSA
  GUID, so `DsReplicaMod` matches that link by address.
- **Netlogon beyond the calls above**: `net ads join` from Samba 4.24.7 now
  completes against this DC. The client gets through the LDAP conversation,
  opens `\pipe\netlogon`, opens its channel
  (`NetrServerReqChallenge` → `NetrServerAuthenticate3` → RID), steps its
  authenticator through `NetrLogonGetCapabilities`, rebinds the pipe with the
  Netlogon security provider and seals every later PDU on it, then writes its
  machine account over LDAP — and exits 0 with
  `Joined 'JOINBOX' to dns domain 'lab.example.org'`. The one step it reports as
  failed is the optional dynamic-DNS update (`NT_STATUS_INVALID_PARAMETER`):
  this DC does not serve DNS updates, and nothing above depends on it. The
  trust channel a partner's DC would open (`NetrServerAuthenticate3` against the
  trust account, then `NetrSetTrustedDomainAuthInfo`) is not served; a partner
  can authenticate its trust channel and read the trust account's RID. The
  client half is proven by `monarchy-netlogon-tests` alongside the real join,
  and the sealed pipe is measured against the bytes Samba sends, not against the
  server's own encoder.
- **SMB beyond the pipe transport**: SMB2 dialects 2.0.2 through 3.1.1, with
  preauth integrity, AES-CMAC signing and AES-128-CCM encryption, but no
  multi-channel, no compression, no AES-256 and no GCM. SMB1 is
  answered for NT LM 0.12 (`engine/Smb1.{h,cpp}`): negotiate, extended session
  setup with NTLMSSP or a Kerberos AP-REQ in SPNEGO, tree connect to `IPC$`,
  NT create on a pipe,
  and write/read/transact over it, which is the transport a Windows 2000
  workstation needs for a domain join. SMB1 signing is implemented: `SecurityMode`
  advertises `SIGNING_ENABLED` (never `SIGNING_REQUIRED`), a signed request is
  verified with MD5(session key || message) before dispatch and refused
  `ACCESS_DENIED` when the MAC does not cover the message, and every reply to a
  signed session carries its own signature over one sequence number shared by
  both directions. That sequence is measured, not assumed: Samba's client logged
  `seq 1: got good` for the session-setup reply, signed its tree connect as 2 and
  expected 3 back. The pre-extended-security session setup (LM and NT responses
  inline, account and domain as terminated strings, no security blob) is
  accepted too, with the responses verified against the challenge the negotiate
  reply advertised — eight zero bytes, since that reply carries none — and the
  SMB1 MAC key built the way the client builds it: the session base key
  followed by the NT response it sent, which is what Samba's
  `smb1_signing_activate` concatenates. The client's own expected MAC then
  reproduces byte for byte, and a signing client over this form lists SYSVOL
  where it was previously refused `smb1_signing_good: BAD SIG: seq 1` — the key
  alone, without the response bytes, is not the key. Live with
  `client signing = required`: `\\dc\SYSVOL` lists,
  and Samba's dcerpc-over-SMB1 transport — which signs every PDU whatever
  `client signing` says — carries SAMR (`enumdomusers`) and Netlogon
  (`getdcname`) over `\pipe\samr` and `\pipe\netlogon`. A client that does not
  sign, such as Windows 2000 with no signing policy, is still answered without a
  signature it never asked for.
  SMB1 also serves the disk shares (`SYSVOL`, `NETLOGON`) the way SMB2 does:
  authenticated tree connect, case-insensitive path resolution, positional
  reads and the TRANS2 query a client makes before reading, with every write,
  mkdir, rename, delete and set-info refused. Directory enumeration is served:
  `TRANS2 FIND_FIRST2`, `FIND_NEXT2` and `FIND_CLOSE2` at information levels
  0x0101 to 0x0104, with a search handle per session so a resumed page returns
  the set the first page resolved, and a bare path that names a directory
  treated as a listing of that directory. `TRANS2 QUERY_FS_INFORMATION` answers
  the volume, size, device and attribute records (0x0102-0x0105); without it a
  Windows 2000 `dir` on an existing share failed with status 50 straight after
  a successful tree connect, because the redirector reads the filesystem
  attributes before it enumerates anything. SMB1's disk shares remain read-only on
either dialect: a GPO is delivered but not edited over the SMB1 path.
  `smbPort` defaults to 445, which needs `CAP_NET_BIND_SERVICE` or root like the
  endpoint mapper.
- **The endpoint mapper's limits**: it binds `epmPort` (135 by default), and
  binding 135 needs `CAP_NET_BIND_SERVICE` or root: an unprivileged console
  reports that on the status bar and the endpoints stay reachable by explicit
  port, which is the same trade a non-root Samba makes. It follows whatever
  address the RPC endpoints bound: `127.0.0.1` while they stay on loopback, and
  `dcIp` — the address this DC publishes for itself — once `rpcAddress` takes
  them off it, so a tower is only ever usable where something answers.
- **`dcIp` is the address clients must use, and the zone publishes it**: the
  DC's own A records and the site's are written from it, so a DC that serves
  other machines and keeps the provisioning default of `127.0.0.1` hands every
  client a locator answer that points at the client's own loopback. That is a
  configuration mistake, not a bug this tree can rule out — provisioning does
  not know which interface is the reachable one — so it is recorded here: set
  `dcIp` to the address a client on another host would use, restart, and the
  swept records follow (`dig @<dcIp> <dcHost> A`). The reply layout is
  verified against impacket's decoder and against Samba's DCE client at bind
  time; Samba's pidl decoder has not been driven through a lookup here (its
  python binding cannot construct the call's `policy_handle`), so an interop
  report from a pidl client would be worth having.
- **DNS, what a server that answers queries does not do here**: EDNS0, reverse
  lookups for the DC's own address and a key-authenticated AXFR are answered
  (§1), but there is no
  **DNSSEC** — a `+dnssec`
  query gets the DO bit echoed and no `RRSIG`, so a validating resolver treats
  every answer as insecure; no **CHAOS** class (`version.bind` is refused, which
  is a choice, and now refused cleanly); and the IXFR history is a journal of the last 64
  changes (§1) kept in the zone file beside the records, so a restart no longer
  costs a client its difference; a client that falls further behind than those 64
  changes is handed the whole zone instead of the delta it asked for. A transfer is gated on the one configured key, and
  `dnsTransferZones=` narrows that key to the zones it may read (§1); there is
  still one key rather than a key per zone, and nothing is gated per name, so a
  key that may transfer the forward zone may transfer every name in it;
  a change is announced to the secondaries named in `dnsNotify=` (§1), repeated
  on a doubling schedule until they answer or five attempts are spent (§1), but
  nothing accepts the secondary side of a transfer — a NOTIFY that arrives here
  is refused, and this server keeps no copy pulled from another primary.
  Zone data is served over UDP/TCP, from the store, with RFC 2136 updates and
  the forwarding resolver above; those are absences, not partial
  implementations.
- **PKINIT** (needs an X.509/CMS path and a CA), **S4U2Self/S4U2Proxy**.
- **Multi-hop/transitive trusts**, transited-realm encoding beyond one hop, SID
  filtering, selective authentication, trust-password rotation.
- **Read authorization from the session's identity** (writes now answer to it:
  the PAC on a Kerberos session, the bound account's groups on a password bind,
  and nothing for Anonymous Logon, with AD's SELF right on `unicodePwd`), PAC

- GPO CSE processing (the shares are writable, but nothing interprets a
  policy's `GPT.INI`/`registry.pol` the way a client's CSE does), multi-DC

## 3. The order of the remaining work, inside a boundary

What this server is for is narrower than AD's feature list, on purpose. The
audience is a homelab, a small business on a tight budget, a family: they
already own a backup product, a monitoring system, a PKI, a DNS and DHCP
server, a hypervisor and a file server, and they should keep them. The boundary
is the directory — LDAP, Kerberos, DRS replication, SMB and NETLOGON, SAMR and
LSAD, the GPO files and the shares that serve them. What already exists as a
mature, freely available product stays out of this tree: backup and restore,
upgrade and migration tooling, monitoring and alerting, a certificate authority
or PKINIT, DHCP, file serving beyond SYSVOL and NETLOGON, ADWS,
AD LDS/CS/FS/RMS, and mail or database glue. DNS is not on that list and is
not an exception to it: a Windows 2000 DC answered DNS itself — the domain's
own zone, forwarding to an upstream resolver, and the cache in front of that
forwarding — and a client that cannot resolve through its logon server is a
client AD does not work for. What stays out is being the network's
general-purpose resolver, or hosting zones nobody asked this DC for. Matching Microsoft's breadth
is not the goal and is the wrong measure — that breadth is how the platform
vendor squeezed out the tools these customers had already chosen, and imitating
it here would put the same squeeze on the same tools. Parity is claimed only
where a Windows DC's behaviour is what makes an existing client or an existing
tool work; a closing item that exists only to make a count match is dropped
rather than built, WINS-era name resolution being the example.

The gaps above are not independent. Working outward from what exists:

1. ~~RPC authentication~~ — NTLMSSP and Kerberos are both in, with signing and
   sealing, and Kerberos shares the KDC's acceptor the LDAP path uses.
2. ~~SAMR writes~~ — create/delete user, group and alias, members in both, every
   information class including the aggregate 21/23/25 writes and the logon
   week, the domain password and lockout policies, the `BUILTIN` domain with
   its sixteen well-known aliases, and every per-field query class
   (`UserAccountInformation` 5, `UserAccountNameInformation` 7,
   `UserPrimaryGroupInformation` 9, home 10, script 11, profile 12, the
   administrative comment 13, `UserWorkStationsInformation` 14, expiry 17 and
   the user parameters 20, alongside the general, preferences and logon records
   1, 2 and 3, now writable as well). Fine-grained password policies are in as
   objects this DC evaluates — `CN=Password Settings Container` under the
   domain, lowest non-zero `msDS-PasswordSettingsPrecedence` wins, omitted
   fields inherit the domain policy, and `msDS-ResultantPSO` is computed for the
   account that asks — and `BUILTIN`'s sixteen well-known aliases are stored
   objects whose membership a client adds and removes, with the client-visible
   SID staying `S-1-5-32-<rid>`.
3. ~~Endpoint mapper~~ — `ept_lookup`/`ept_map` answer on `epmPort`, so a client
   can now discover a port instead of being told one.
4. ~~SMB2 server + named pipes + SYSVOL/NETLOGON shares~~ — `\\dc\pipe\samr`,
   `\pipe\lsarpc`, `\pipe\netlogon` and `\pipe\epmapper` carry DCE/RPC into the
   same endpoints the TCP ports serve, and `\\dc\SYSVOL` / `\\dc\NETLOGON` serve
   the domain's GPOs and logon scripts to authenticated sessions — writable for
   the accounts that administer the domain. The dialects through 3.1.1 sign and
   seal: an encrypted session is opened by Samba's `smbclient` and by
   `smbprotocol` at both 3.1.1 and 3.0.2, and `scripts/smb-conformance.py`
   measures it.
5. ~~LSAD~~ — the policy calls, both translation directions, account rights,
   privileges, secrets and the policy security descriptor, verified against
   Samba's reference NDR decoder and impacket by `scripts/lsad-conformance.py`.
   Remaining here: trust-password rotation and the trust channel's
   `NetrSetTrustedDomainAuthInfo` write.
6. ~~Netlogon~~ — secure channel, machine-password write, SamLogon validation
   and the DC locator calls are served and verified (§1). The locator's
   **CLDAP ping over UDP** is answered too: both shapes real clients send —
   Samba's `NtVer`-only ping and the Windows 2000 `DsGetDcName` ping that names the domain with its root dot and carries the client's Host/User/AAC/DomainSid/DomainGuid —
   are served and driven by `scripts/cldap-conformance.py`, which pins Samba's
   own datagram from a capture. The binary `Netlogon` attribute
   (`NETLOGON_SAM_LOGON_RESPONSE_EX`) is answered too, which is the only thing
   Samba's `net ads lookup` reads: it now prints this DC's record — the domain
   GUID, the PDC/GC/LDAP/DS/KDC/writable/closest flags with no time service
   claimed, forest and domain names, both site names, `NT 5` and the two
   `0xffff` tokens. The blob is mask-independent (nt_version `5` inside it says
   there is no address field, so no mask can make one appear), the entry carries
   the `Netlogon` attribute and nothing else whatever attributes the ping named
   — a Windows 2000 DC answers that way (measured against one), Samba's locator
   rejects any entry that is not exactly one attribute, and a named list is a
   capability hint rather than a filter over the answer; echoing it back was
   this server's own behaviour turned into a test, and both the answer and the
   conformance script now follow the measured one. Three more differences were measured against that DC and are served here: a ping asking for WITH_IP gets the address the datagram arrived on and a trailing word that says so (`0x0d` with the address, `0x05` without); a ping whose domain assertion names another domain matches nothing and is answered success with no entry, which is what the filter evaluation in RFC 4511 §4.5.3 gives and what the real DC sends (22 bytes, the result alone); and a UDP search that is not a ping is refused `unwillingToPerform` with AD's own diagnostic instead of being left silent — and the reply is two
   LDAPMessages, not one SEQUENCE wrapping both, which is how a client reads an
   id followed by a result. That framing bug was invisible until a real client
   was driven against the server: its own driver read the wrong shape happily. A real member's join flow and
   RPC-layer schannel are done (§6); what remains here is trust creation over
   the channel.
7. ~~DRSR~~ — `DsBind`/`DsGetNCChanges`/`DsReplicaSync` plus `DsCrackNames`
   and `DsGetDomainControllerInfo` are served and verified (§1). Both halves of
   replication run: `ReplicationAgent` takes a partner from `dc.conf` or a wire
   `DsReplicaAdd` on a timer and pulls all three naming contexts — domain,
   configuration and schema — with a cursor each, verified against two
   provisioned DCs (§1). So what remains is the KCC's topology, and proving the
   path against Samba or Windows peers, which is what makes a domain a domain
   rather than one server.

After those: GPO processing, FSMO, sites and the KCC, interdomain trust
replication, WINS-era name resolution if 2000 parity is taken literally, and the
schema/LDF import path. Each block is landed the way SAMR was: against the
IDL in `reference/specs/`, driven by an independent client, with the CI suite
(`scripts/ci.sh`, which `.github/workflows/ci.yml` runs) extended in the same
change.

## 4. Security posture

Development directory service, not a hardened DC. LDAP is plaintext unless the
client asks for StartTLS (there is no dedicated LDAPS port, and no client
certificate is requested), and an unbound session is answered the way Windows 2000
answers it: the read is served, because `restrictAnonymous` in `dc.conf` defaults
to 0 and a 2000 DC restricts anonymous reads only to what `Anonymous Logon` may
see (`1` is AD's Server 2003 default, where a search with a non-empty base answers
`insufficientAccessRights(50)`). Password material never leaves the server at
either level, a failed bind leaves the connection anonymous, and StartTLS resets
it (RFC 4511 §4.14.1).
DNS updates and zone transfers can be authenticated with a TSIG key (RFC 8945,
hmac-sha256) when `dnsTsigName`/`dnsTsigSecret` are set in `dc.conf` — an
unsigned, wrong-key or wrong-MAC message is then refused `NOTAUTH` with the
matching TSIG error, an accepted update is answered signed, and an AXFR is
answered with the zone only once the MAC verifies, and a NOTIFY announcing a
moved serial is signed with the same key — but without a key updates
stay open while every transfer is refused, and there is no per-name or per-zone
ACL either way, so one key both writes and reads the whole zone; RPC has no
authorization model beyond "the bind authenticated": any account that
authenticates can administer. The RPC endpoints therefore bind `127.0.0.1`
only (`samrPort` 1035, `lsadPort` 1036 and `drsPort` 1038 by default). Do not
expose ports 1389/1053/1035/1036/1038 to untrusted networks, and do not move
the RPC endpoints off loopback until per-account RPC authorization exists.

`rpcAddress` in `dc.conf` is how that decision is made deliberately: unset it
and the endpoints stay on loopback; set it to `0.0.0.0`, or to this host's
address, and SAMR, LSAD, Netlogon, DRSR and the endpoint mapper serve the
network — which is what a domain join from another machine needs, and it hands
every account in the domain administrative reach over RPC. Provisioning leaves
it unset.
